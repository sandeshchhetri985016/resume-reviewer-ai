# Aviation Resume Evaluation Guidance
# Use this to evaluate and provide feedback on aviation resumes.

## Key Resume Strengths to Look For
- Flight hours and type ratings with specific aircraft models
- Safety record metrics (incident-free hours, safety awards)
- Licenses and ratings (CASA, FAA, EASA, ATPL, CPL, PPL, IR, ME)
- Crew coordination and leadership experience
- Regulatory compliance knowledge (CASR, CAR, ICAO Annexes)
- Simulation and training experience

## Metrics That Impress Aviation Employers
- Total flight hours (PIC, dual, instrument, night)
- Years of incident-free operations
- Aircraft types endorsed and total hours per type
- Training throughput (e.g., "Delivered ground school to 50+ cadets")
- Check ride pass rates
- Passengers or cargo transported statistics

## Key Areas to Evaluate
### Licenses & Ratings
- CASA License (ATPL, CPL, PPL) with class ratings
- Instrument Rating, Multi-Engine Command
- Type Ratings (Boeing, Airbus, Dash-8, etc.)
- Instructor Ratings (Grade 1/2/3, AFF, IRE, TRE)
- Medical Class 1/2 current
- Dangerous goods, CRM, TEM, SMS training

### Experience Types
- Airline operations (regular public transport)
- Charter and corporate aviation
- Flight instruction and training
- Agricultural, aerial survey, or specialized operations
- Military aviation experience
- Remote pilot (drone / RPA) operations

### Non-Technical Skills
- Crew resource management (CRM) examples
- Decision-making under pressure
- Communication with ATC and crew
- Fatigue management understanding
- Passenger service and emergency handling

## Common Resume Mistakes in Aviation
- Flight hours not broken down by category (PIC, dual, instrument, etc.)
- Missing license numbers, ratings, or medical expiry dates
- No type ratings or aircraft-specific hours
- Safety record not mentioned
- Gaps in flying history unexplained
- Instructor experience not quantified (students trained, check rides)

## Resume Structure Recommendations
1. Professional Summary (total hours, licenses, key experience)
2. Licenses & Ratings (with license numbers and expiry dates)
3. Flight Experience (hours by category: PIC, Co-pilot, Instrument, Night, etc.)
4. Professional Experience (airline/operator, aircraft type, role, dates)
5. Training & Instruction (if applicable)
6. Education (aviation degree, certifications)
7. Additional: Medical class, security clearances, language proficiency
